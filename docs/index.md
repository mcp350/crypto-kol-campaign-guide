# Crypto KOL campaign checklist

A working checklist for token teams, exchanges and Web3 products booking KOLs on X, Telegram and YouTube.

It follows a campaign in order: vet, price, sequence, format, measure. Each step links to a longer write-up.

Maintained by [3betmedia](https://3betmedia.com/), a creator-led growth agency in Vilnius. We have run crypto KOL campaigns since 2018.

## 1. Know who you are booking

A KOL, a KOC and an influencer do different jobs. A KOL moves opinion. A KOC is a real user who posts about the product. An influencer sells reach.

- [What is a KOL in crypto](https://3betmedia.com/insights/what-is-a-kol-in-crypto/)
- [What is a KOC in crypto](https://3betmedia.com/insights/what-is-a-koc-in-crypto/)
- [Crypto KOL vs influencer](https://3betmedia.com/insights/crypto-kol-vs-influencer/)

## 2. Vet before you book

Wallets are public. So is a creator's history of calls. Check both before a contract goes out.

- Does the KOL buy before the post and sell into it?
- Do replies come from many accounts or the same handful?
- Did followers jump more than 30% in a month?
- Are more than half of recent posts paid promos?

Full method: [crypto KOL due diligence checklist](https://3betmedia.com/insights/crypto-kol-due-diligence/) and [vetting KOLs by their wallets](https://3betmedia.com/insights/crypto-kol-tracker/).

Split the fee into its lines so you can see what each part buys.

## 4. Sequence the launch

Who posts first matters as much as who posts.

Callers, community accounts and explainers do different jobs in the first 48 hours. Plan the order before anyone gets a brief.

## 6. Measure what came back

Decide the measure before launch, not after.

Good ones: swaps in the 30 minutes after a post, holders at day seven, sign-ups by tagged link.
